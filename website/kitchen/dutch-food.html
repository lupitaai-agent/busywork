Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

Dutch and Flemish food: the dishes people cook at home

Butter, bread and a warm meal at six.

🍳 Open Busy Kitchen free

Pictures made with AI, to show the kitchen's places and customs.

The Dutch-language kitchen has three layers. In the north it is sober and generous at once: potatoes, dairy from the wet green meadows, rye bread and herring. In the south, in Brabant, Limburg and Flanders, it is "Burgundian", named after the dukes whose court loved a feast: stews, tarts and fries with a proper sauce. And through history and migration, the Indische and Surinamese kitchens became everyday Dutch food: the rijsttafel, roti, pom and bami.

The Low Countries cooked with butter where others used lard or oil. Ordinary people ate porridge and thick soups, the ancestors of today's snert. In the fourteenth century a way of preserving herring built a trade that fed half of Europe. In the Golden Age, Dutch ships brought nutmeg, cloves and cinnamon home, and cheaper spices gave the Netherlands speculaas.

The day still follows bread: twice a day, with cheese, sprinkles or peanut butter. The one warm meal comes at six: potatoes, vegetables and a little meat, with gravy in a well in the stamppot. Coffee comes with one biscuit. A birthday means sitting in a circle; a new baby means rusks with pink or blue sprinkles.

How to say it

stamppot (STAMP-pot), oliebollen (OH-lee-bol-len), speculaas (SPAY-kew-lahs).

Its regions

  • Netherlands, Northeast (Groningen, Friesland, Drenthe, Overijssel, northern Gelderland) Rye bread, cured and dried sausage (metworst, Gelderse rookworst), balkenbrij, eierbal, spekdik, kruidkoek (Groninger koek), suikerbrood, Fryske dúmkes, poffert; Friese nagelkaas and kanterkaas. Kniepertjes (New Year wafers) belong to Drenthe, Groningen, Twente, the Achterhoek and Salland. Groninger mosterdsoep and the eierbal are Groningen icons. Fryske sûkerbôle, oranjekoek and dúmkes stand for Friesland.
  • Netherlands, West (Noord- and Zuid-Holland, Utrecht, Zeeland, the Betuwe) Butter-rich biscuits, stroopwafels, gevulde koek, oliebollen, Zeeuwse bolus, duivekater (Zaanstreek); Gouda, Edam and Leidse kaas; herring, Zeeuwse mosselen, eel, oysters, shrimp; ossenworst with Amsterdamse uitjes; bitterballen and kroketten. Leiden has hutspot on 3 October. West-Friesland has the broeder (Jan in de zak). Zeeland is known for mussels, oysters, bolussen and babbelaars, with Yerseke as the shellfish centre.
  • Netherlands, South (Noord-Brabant, Limburg) The "Bourgondisch" style: steaks and tenderloin with sauce, hachee, vegetable soup with meatballs, asparagus and witlof, butcher-made croquettes, Limburgse vlaai, Bossche bol, Brabantse worstenbroodje, appelstroop. Limburg adds zoervleis with fries, nonnevotten at carnival, and Mestreechter stroop. North Limburg is the largest asparagus area in the Netherlands Brabantse Wal asparagus is EU-protected.
  • Flevoland A young polder province (land from the 1940s–60s) with no distinct dish canon found. Urk, a former island, has a fishing tradition.
  • Flanders West-Vlaanderen: potjesvlees (Westhoek), garnaalkroketten (Oostende), hennepot (Poperinge), hopscheuten, West-Vlaamse lukken. Oost-Vlaanderen: Gentse waterzooi, stoverij, Tierenteyn mustard, cuberdons, mastellen, hoofdvlees (Gent) mattentaart (Geraardsbergen), Aalsterse vlaai, Lokerse paardenworst. Antwerpen: Antwerpse handjes, paling in 't groen, Liers vlaaike, Kempen kipkap and balkenbrij. Vlaams-Brabant and Brussels: stoemp, Brusselse wafel, speculoos, pain à la grecque, grondwitloof, kip met frieten en appelmoes, Hoegaardse suikervlaai, tafeldruiven of Overijse. Belgian Limburg: Limburgse vlaai (shared PGI with the Netherlands), Hasseltse speculaas, Limburgse stroop, knapkoek.
  • Liège and Wallonia (outside the Dutch-language area but in every Flemish kitchen) Luikse wafel, Luikse siroop, boulets à la liégeoise, salade liégeoise, smoutebollen at the Foire de Liège. Boudin blanc de Liège became an EU PGI in November 2025.
  • Suriname in the Netherlands Creole (pom, moksi alesi, bojo, telo), Hindustani (roti, bara, massala chicken), Javanese (saoto, bami, nasi, dawet), Chinese (moksi meti, tjauw min), Jewish (pom, fiadoe, orgeade). Restaurants and shops are concentrated in the Randstad; Javanese places are often called warungs.
  • Indisch in the Netherlands Home cooking of Indische families (rendang, sambal goreng, sajoer lodeh, spekkoek), tokos, the rijsttafel, and the Chinese-Indische restaurant.

How people eat

  • Broodmaaltijd twice a day Breakfast and lunch are mainly bread: butter or margarine, cheese, cold cuts, jam, peanut butter, hagelslag. Hot lunches are uncommon.
  • Warm eten om zes uur Most people eat one warm meal a day, around 18:00, often followed by a toetje such as vla or yoghurt. The classic plate is AVG: potatoes, vegetables, a small piece of meat with gravy. Gravy goes in a kuiltje (well) in the stamppot.
  • Weekday rhythm Wednesday was gehaktdag (meatball day) Friday was traditionally fish day. The armed forces serve the Indische "Blauwe hap" on Wednesdays.
  • Koffie met een koekje Coffee around 10–11 and tea around 16:00 one biscuit per cup, a custom tied to post-war thrift. In Limburg a large vlaai is cut into eight pieces.
  • Borrel Late-afternoon drinks with bitterballen and mustard, vlammetjes, borrelnootjes, cheese cubes.
  • Verjaardag in de kring At birthdays guests sit in a circle in the living room and get coffee with cake, then drinks and snacks.
  • Kraamvisite Visitors to a new baby get beschuit met muisjes: pink and white for a girl, blue and white for a boy. Orange muisjes were offered to the royal house in 1938 for the birth of Princess Beatrix.
  • Funerals Sober: coffee with boterkoek or cake. In Brabant the worstenbroodje is a common part of the funeral koffietafel. In Belgium soft "sandwiches" (sweet bread rolls) are eaten on Sundays and after funerals.
  • Weddings Bitterkoekjes are served at weddings.
  • Rijsttafel All dishes go on the table at once; each guest builds a plate around rice. It is served at family days, reunions and memorials, and being together matters as much as the food.
  • Surinamese feasts "No party without pom" is a common saying; pom is served at birthdays and festivals. Roti is served when many guests come. Bojo is eaten at Keti Koti and at births, deaths and weddings.
  • Flanders Fries come from the frietkot or frituur, in a paper cone. Stoofvlees-friet won a VRT vote as the ultimate Flemish classic. Brasserie classics (vol-au-vent, balletjes in tomatensaus, tomaat-garnaal) are also cooked at home.
  • Skating On natural ice, koek-en-zopie stalls sell hot chocolate, pea soup and cake anijsmelk was the traditional skating drink.

In the cupboard

  • Potatoes The base of stamppot and fries. Opperdoezer Ronde (Noord-Holland) is an EU PDO potato.
  • Winter vegetables Boerenkool (curly kale), endive, sauerkraut, red cabbage, carrots, onions, brown beans, split peas, capucijners.
  • Spring vegetables White asparagus (Brabantse Wal PDO), raapstelen, Poperingse hopscheuten (PGI).
  • Witlof/witloof (Belgian endive) Found in Brussels in the 1850s; Brussels grondwitloof is a PGI.
  • Dairy Butter, buttermilk (karnemelk), vla, yoghurt, quark; cheeses Gouda Holland and Edam Holland (PGI), Noord-Hollandse Gouda and Edammer (PDO), Boeren-Leidse met sleutels (PDO), Hollandse geitenkaas (PGI), boerenkaas (TSG). Cheese is graded by age, from jong (about 4 weeks) to overjarig (1–2 years).
  • Pork and sausage Rookworst, metworst, spek (bacon), balkenbrij, blood sausage, hoofdkaas/kopvlees.
  • Fish Herring (Hollandse Nieuwe TSG), eel, mussels and oysters from Yerseke, North Sea grey shrimp, cod, plaice.
  • Sweeteners and spreads Basterdsuiker and suikerstroop (both TSG), appelstroop, perenstroop, kandijsuiker, Luikse siroop.
  • Spices Cinnamon, nutmeg, clove, ginger, anise (speculaas, ontbijtkoek, muisjes), a legacy of the VOC spice trade.
  • Mustard Groninger mosterd, Zaanse mosterd, Tierenteyn (Gent).
  • Rye and buckwheat Roggebrood, Fries roggebrood (baked up to 20 hours), buckwheat in balkenbrij and pancakes.
  • Indische pantry Ketjap, sambal oelek, trassi, santen (coconut milk), sereh, laos, kroepoek.
  • Surinamese pantry Pomtajer (Xanthosoma), cassava, bakbanaan, kousenband, zoutvlees, bakkeljauw (salt cod), Madame Jeanette pepper, massala.

Through the year

  • 1 January Stamppot snijbonen in some regions; kniepertjes in the Northeast; Flemish children read New Year's letters
  • 6 January, Driekoningen Driekoningentaart with a bean or figurine (Flanders, growing in West-Vlaanderen); derthienkoeck at the Groot Begijnhof in Leuven
  • 2 February, Lichtmis Pancakes (Belgium)
  • Carnival (Feb/Mar) Nonnevotten (Limburg, sold from about a month before until Ash Wednesday); zoervleis with fries; haringsalade at the end
  • Halfvasten (mid-Lent) Mastellen in Gent
  • Wednesday before Easter Herring handed out in Niekerk, Oldekerk and Faan (Groningen), recorded since 1476
  • Pasen Paasstol, chocolate eggs
  • Spring (Mar–Jun) Raapstelen; Poperingse hopscheuten; white asparagus
  • 27 April, Koningsdag Oranje tompouce
  • Herring season Hollandse Nieuwe: catch 1 May–31 Aug per the TSG file; first barrel and Vlaggetjesdag in Scheveningen (since 1781)
  • 30 May Zeeuwse Bolusdag (since 2015)
  • 1 July, Keti Koti Surinamese food: bojo, telo met pindasaus, pom
  • Summer Schaafijs; aardbeien met beschuit
  • Kermis (fair season) Poffertjes, oliebollen, smoutebollen, kermiskoek; lacquemants at Antwerp's Sinksenfoor
  • August Mastellen at the Gentse Patersholfeesten; hoofdvlees at the Gentse Feesten
  • 3 October, Leidens Ontzet Hutspot, herring and white bread
  • Autumn Game, hazenpeper
  • 11 November, Sint-Maarten Sweets for children (Utrecht, Denderstreek and Ieper area in Flanders)
  • 5 December (NL) / 6 December (BE) Speculaas, taaitaai, pepernoten, kruidnoten, borstplaat, chocoladeletter, banketletter, marsepein; klaaskoeken in Flanders
  • Kerst Kerststol at brunch, kerstkransjes, rollade, gourmetten, stoofperen, tulband; cougnou in Belgium
  • 31 December, Oud en Nieuw Oliebollen, appelbeignets, appelflappen, ananasbeignets
  • Winter on the ice Snert, chocolademelk, anijsmelk, koek-en-zopie
