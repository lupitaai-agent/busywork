Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

Portuguese and Brazilian food: the dishes people cook at home

The sea, the cassava and three roots.

🍳 Open Busy Kitchen free

Pictures made with AI, to show the kitchen's places and customs.

The Portuguese-language kitchen joins Portugal and Brazil. Portugal is the sea and salt cod, the old spice route, olive oil and bread, and the convents whose nuns turned egg yolks and sugar into gold: ovos moles, pastéis de nata and many sweets with heavenly names.

Brazil grew from three roots. The Indigenous base is cassava, made into farinha, and the techniques of smoking, earth ovens and pirão. African cooks brought dendê palm oil and dishes such as acarajé, whose sellers in Bahia are national heritage. The Portuguese brought their stews of beans and pork. Later, Italian, German, Arab and Japanese families added their own; the first Japanese immigrants arrived in 1908. Feijoada is often said to have been invented in the slave quarters, but the earliest Brazilian record is an 1827 advertisement, and its roots are in Portuguese bean stews.

Lunch is the main meal in both countries. In Brazil it is rice and beans with something on the side; on Saturday, feijoada; in the south, churrasco with a gourd of chimarrão passed round. In Portugal lunch often starts with soup and lasts over an hour, and petiscos are shared at the end of the day.

How to say it

feijoada (fay-zhoo-AH-dah), pão de queijo (pown jee KAY-zhoo), bacalhau (bah-kah-LYOW).

Its regions

  • North (Pará, Amazonas, Acre, Rondônia, Roraima, Amapá, Tocantins) The kitchen is mostly Indigenous with a Portuguese overlay. Key flavours are tucupi, jambu, river fish (tambaqui, pirarucu), açaí, cupuaçu, bacuri and farinha d'água. Signature dishes are tacacá, pato no tucupi, maniçoba, pirarucu de casaca, vatapá paraense and the x-caboquinho of Manaus. Belém's Círio de Nazaré lunch is the region's great feast.
  • Centre-West (Goiás, Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Distrito Federal) Goiás eats pequi, pamonha, galinhada and empadão, with strong influence from Minas. Mato Grosso has Pantanal fish (pacu, pintado, piranha), Maria Isabel and bolo de arroz. Mato Grosso do Sul shows Paraguayan, Bolivian, Japanese and Indigenous influence: sopa paraguaia, chipa, saltenha, sobá, tereré.
  • Southeast (Minas Gerais, São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Espírito Santo) Minas: pão de queijo, queijo minas, tutu, feijão tropeiro, pork and maize, fruit sweets. São Paulo: virado à paulista, cuscuz paulista, pastel, the immigrant pizza, esfiha and sushi culture. Rio: feijoada, filé à Oswaldo Aranha, bolinho de bacalhau, mate gelado. Espírito Santo: moqueca capixaba and torta capixaba.
  • South (Paraná, Santa Catarina, Rio Grande do Sul) The gaúcho kitchen of beef, churrasco and chimarrão is shared with Argentina and Uruguay. Italian and German colonies have polenta, galeto, cuca and chimia. Paraná has barreado and pinhão. Santa Catarina's coast keeps an Azorean fish-and-shrimp tradition. Pelotas has fine sweets of Portuguese descent.
  • Minho and Douro Litoral (Porto) Caldo verde, rojões, sarrabulho, broa, Porto's tripas and francesinha, bacalhau à Gomes de Sá, Pudim Abade de Priscos. Bread rolls (bolas) are served with sardines and meat.
  • Trás-os-Montes and Alto Douro Alheira, the cured meats of Vinhais and Barroso (many PGIs), Mirandesa beef, chestnuts, folar of Chaves and Valpaços, pastel de Chaves, the cristas de galo of Vila Real.
  • Beiras (Centro) Leitão da Bairrada, chanfana, Serra da Estrela cheese, Aveiro's ovos moles, Tentúgal pastries, pão-de-ló de Ovar, sopa da Beira and migas.
  • Lisboa, Estremadura, Ribatejo and Setúbal Pastéis de Belém, sardines, bacalhau à Brás, amêijoas à Bulhão Pato, Sintra's queijadas and travesseiros, Azeitão cheese, Setúbal's choco frito, Almeirim's sopa da pedra and caralhotas, the fatias of Tomar.
  • Alentejo A bread-based kitchen of açordas and migas, pork from Alentejo black pigs (carne de porco alentejano DOP), lamb, coriander, mint, oregano, sericaia and pão de rala.
  • Algarve Almond and fig sweets (doces finos, Dom Rodrigo, morgado), cataplana, xarém com conquilhas, choquinhos à algarvia.
  • Açores Cozido das Furnas, alcatra of Terceira, sopas do Espírito Santo, São Jorge and Pico cheeses, queijadas of Graciosa, PDO pineapple and passion fruit.
  • Madeira Espetada on bay-laurel skewers, bolo do caco, bolo de mel, scabbard fish with banana, milho frito, carne vinha d'alhos.

How people eat

  • Brazil, the day's meals Café da manhã is light: coffee with milk, bread, fruit, cake, cheese, requeijão. There may be a mid-morning lanche. Almoço, from 11:00 to 14:00, is the main meal: rice and beans, salad, a protein, a side. An afternoon lanche or café da tarde follows, and jantar is often light (soup, sandwich, pasta). The ceia is a late supper tied to Christmas and New Year. Coffee is the national drink, usually sweet.
  • Brazil, weekly and festive rituals Feijoada is a Wednesday or Saturday lunch in Rio, São Paulo, Espírito Santo and Minas. It is served with rice, sautéed couve, farofa and orange slices. Churrasco is the South's main dish and the default family and friends gathering, with farofa and maionese. Children's birthdays mean brigadeiro, beijinho and cajuzinho. Chimarrão is shared in a circle from one gourd. Tereré, the cold version, is shared the same way in Mato Grosso do Sul. The Sunday family lunch (almoço de domingo) is a widely described custom, but no fetched source documents it.
  • Portugal, the day's meals Breakfast is bread with butter, ham or cheese and coffee or milk, and a pastel de nata is common. Lunch runs from about 12:00 to 14:00, typically around 13:00. It often lasts over an hour, has three courses and usually opens with soup. Dinner is around 20:00, also often with soup. In the afternoon people take pastries and coffee. "Café" means espresso, and in Lisbon a bica. Coffee-house orders include galão, meia de leite, garoto, pingado and abatanado.
  • Portugal, petiscos Petiscos are small shared plates: caracóis, pataniscas, peixinhos da horta, amêijoas à Bulhão Pato, choco frito, bifanas. The 2021 "7 Maravilhas da Nova Gastronomia" had a whole Petiscos category.
  • Portugal, national recognition Since 2015, Portugal has had a Dia Nacional da Gastronomia Portuguesa on the last Sunday of May. The 2011 public vote "7 Maravilhas da Gastronomia" chose alheira de Mirandela, Queijo Serra da Estrela, caldo verde, sardinha assada, arroz de marisco, leitão da Bairrada and pastel de Belém.

In the cupboard

  • Brazil, starches and grains Mandioca/macaxeira/aipim and everything made from it (farinha de mandioca, farinha d'água, goma/tapioca, polvilho doce and azedo, tucupi, maniva); maize (fubá, flocão, canjica, milho verde); rice; beans (carioca, preto, fradinho, verde/de-corda).
  • Brazil, fats and liquids Dendê, coconut milk and grated coconut, manteiga de garrafa (clarified butter).
  • Brazil, proteins Carne de sol, charque/carne-seca; pork (toucinho, linguiça, torresmo); goat in the sertão; river fish (tambaqui, pirarucu, pacu, pintado); shrimp (fresh and dried), crab, sururu.
  • Brazil, greens and seasonings Couve, quiabo (okra), jambu, vinagreira; cheiro-verde (parsley and spring onion), coentro, pimenta-de-cheiro, pimenta malagueta, urucum (annatto).
  • Brazil, fruits and nuts Pequi, pinhão, açaí, cupuaçu, caju, guaraná, maracujá, goiaba (as goiabada).
  • Brazil, dairy and sugar Queijo minas and coalho, condensed milk (brigadeiro, pudim), rapadura and cane sugar.
  • Portugal, staples and fats Olive oil (eight PDO oils), garlic, bay leaf, coriander (Alentejo), parsley, paprika (colorau), piri-piri; bread (broa of maize and rye, Alentejo wheat bread), carolino rice.
  • Portugal, meat and fish Cured pork (chouriço, salpicão, alheira, farinheira, presunto, morcela: dozens of PGIs); salt cod, sardines, octopus, clams, cuttlefish.
  • Portugal, vegetables, nuts and fruit Couve galega, potatoes, chickpeas and beans; almonds and chestnuts; Elvas plums.
  • Portugal, dairy, eggs and spices Sheep's and goat's cheeses (Serra da Estrela, Azeitão, Évora, Nisa, Serpa, São Jorge, Pico); egg yolks and sugar for convent sweets; cinnamon, cloves and allspice (alcatra).
