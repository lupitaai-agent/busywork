Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

Christmas food in six kitchens

From Advent and Sinterklaas to Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and Epiphany: the days six kitchens celebrate, and the dishes they cook for them.

🍳 Open Busy Kitchen free

The days

  • 🇳🇱 Kerst Kerststol at brunch, kerstkransjes, rollade, gourmetten, stoofperen, tulband; cougnou in Belgium
  • 🇩🇪 Advent Plätzchen baking; Stollen, Lebkuchen, Printen, Spekulatius, Springerle; Christmas markets (Striezelmarkt Dresden, Christkindlesmarkt Nürnberg); Kinderpunsch
  • 🇪🇸 All December Turrón, polvorones, mantecados, mazapán, marquesitas, neules, cordiales, guirlache, alfajores
  • 🇬🇧 Last Sunday before Advent Stir-up Sunday: Christmas puddings made at home
  • 🇬🇧 December Mince pies, Christmas cake, spiced beef (Cork)
  • 🇳🇱 5 December (NL) / 6 December (BE) Speculaas, taaitaai, pepernoten, kruidnoten, borstplaat, chocoladeletter, banketletter, marsepein; klaaskoeken in Flanders
  • 🇩🇪 6 Dec, Nikolaus Stutenkerl and Weckmann (DE), Grittibänz (CH), nuts, mandarins, Lebkuchen
  • 🇮🇹 13 Dec Panonta (Molise); occhi di Santa Lucia (Bari). Sicilian arancine and cuccìa on this day:
  • 🇪🇸 16–24 Dec Novena (Colombia), posadas (Mexico)
  • 🇪🇸 21 Dec Talo con txistorra
  • 🇩🇪 24 Dec, Heiligabend Kartoffelsalat mit Würstchen (most common in DE); carp (Austria, Bavaria, the East); fondue chinoise in Switzerland
  • 🇪🇸 24 Dec Seafood, besugo, sopa de almendras, cardo, caracoles (Cantabria), bacalao con coliflor (Galicia), besugo en salsa (La Rioja)
  • 🇪🇸 24–25 Dec Nochebuena and Navidad
  • 🇮🇹 24 Dec Meatless feast: capitone and baccalà (Naples, Lazio, Calabria), spaghetti alle vongole, cappon magro (Genoa), bigoli in salsa (Veneto), tortelli di zucca (Mantua), pasta with breadcrumbs and anchovies (south), culurgiones de casu (Sardinia), zengarielle (Molise)
  • 🇧🇷 24–25 December Ceia de Natal: peru or chester, tender, farofa, salpicão, maionese, bacalhoada, rabanada, panetone, fruits; leitão in Minas
  • 🇩🇪 25–26 Dec Goose, duck, Rouladen, game, Klöße, red cabbage; Christstollen with coffee (c for poultry dominance)
  • 🇪🇸 25 Dec Escudella i carn d'olla, puchero, putxero de Nadal, roast lamb, suckling pig, capon, caldo con pelotas
  • 🇮🇹 25–26 Dec Tortellini or cappelletti in broth, agnolotti, natalini, roast capon, bollito; panettone, pandoro, panforte, struffoli, cartellate, pandolce, gubana, zelten
  • 🇬🇧 25 December Christmas dinner (turkey, ham in Ireland)
  • 🇪🇸 26 Dec Canelons
  • 🇬🇧 26 December Boxing Day (St Stephen's Day in Ireland): cold cuts, bubble and squeak, turkey curry, pork pie
  • 🇳🇱 31 December, Oud en Nieuw Oliebollen, appelbeignets, appelflappen, ananasbeignets
  • 🇩🇪 31 Dec, Silvester Raclette or Fondue, Berliner (with a mustard-filled joke one), carp, herring salad, Bleigießen (custom, not food)
  • 🇪🇸 31 Dec Twelve grapes at the midnight chimes, recorded in the press by 1882 and popularised by the 1909 Alicante grape surplus
  • 🇧🇷 31 December Lentils at the Réveillon
  • 🇬🇧 31 December Hogmanay
  • 🇮🇹 1 Jan (and 31 Dec night) Cotechino or zampone with lentils (north, Emilia, Lombardy); lentil soup (Tuscany); sciusci fried rings on 31 Dec (Molise)
  • 🇪🇸 5–6 Jan Roscón de Reyes, eaten on the afternoon of 5 January during the Cabalgata or on 6 January, with a hidden figurine; Valencian casca; Asturian puff-pastry version
  • 🇳🇱 6 January, Driekoningen Driekoningentaart with a bean or figurine (Flanders, growing in West-Vlaanderen); derthienkoeck at the Groot Begijnhof in Leuven
  • 🇩🇪 6 Jan, Dreikönigstag Dreikönigskuchen (CH): revived from 1952 by bread researcher Max Währen, with a plastic king hidden inside
  • 🇪🇸 6 Jan Día de Reyes (Mexico and others)
  • 🇮🇹 6 Jan Ciciu 'd capdan bread dolls (Cuneo); the end of the Christmas sweets season
  • 🇧🇷 6 January (Reis) Folia de Reis (no food source fetched)
  • 🇬🇧 6 January Twelfth Night (historic Twelfth cake, ancestor of the Christmas cake)

The dishes

🇳🇱 Dutch and Flemish food

  • Rodekool met appeltjes · Red cabbage with apples

    Where NL – national

    When Winter, Christmas

    A standard side dish.

    Key ingredients Red cabbage, apples, clove, vinegar

    How it is eaten Hot, next to meat

  • Duivekater · Duivekater bread

    Where NL – Zaanstreek, Noord-Holland

    When Christmas, also Easter and Pentecost

    A rich white bread flavoured with lemon zest, baked in a decorated diamond shape in the Zaanstreek; it appears in paintings by Jan Steen.

    Key ingredients Enriched white dough

    How it is eaten With butter

  • Stoofperen · Stewed pears

    Where NL – national

    When Winter, Christmas

    Eaten as a warm vegetable at Christmas or as dessert.

    Key ingredients Gieser Wildeman pears, red wine or juice, cinnamon

    How it is eaten Warm or cold

  • Speculaas · Spiced biscuit

    Where NL and BE – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Cheaper Golden Age spices made it common. Name may come from klaaskoeken or Latin speculator.

    Key ingredients Flour, brown sugar, speculaas spices

    How it is eaten With coffee; as pop figures

    Protected VLAM label: several Belgian speculaas

  • Gevulde speculaas · Almond-filled speculaas

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    A Sinterklaas variant.

    Key ingredients Speculaas dough, almond paste

    How it is eaten Slices with coffee

  • Taaitaai · Chewy anise gingerbread

    Where NL – national

    When 5–6 Dec

    Sold as figures ("vrijers") in Sinterklaas time.

    Key ingredients Rye flour, honey, anise

    How it is eaten Figures for children

  • Pepernoten · Small spiced cookies

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Small irregular cookies; thrown by the Pieten.

    Key ingredients Rye, honey, anise

    How it is eaten By the handful

  • Kruidnoten · Spiced cookie bites

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Many modern variants (chocolate, filled).

    Key ingredients Flour, sugar, speculaas spices

    How it is eaten By the handful

  • Borstplaat · Fondant discs

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Called the oldest Dutch sweet (16th c.); once also used as a cold remedy.

    Key ingredients Sugar, butter, cream, flavour

    How it is eaten Small discs or hearts

  • Chocoladeletter · Chocolate letter

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Became affordable for many families in the 20th century. The Verkade wartime story is doubted.

    Key ingredients Chocolate

    How it is eaten The first letter of your name

  • Banketletter / boterletter · Almond-paste letter

    Where NL – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Often an S shape; has a long history beside the bread letter.

    Key ingredients Puff pastry, almond paste

    How it is eaten Slices with coffee

  • Marsepein · Marzipan figures

    Where NL and BE – national

    When Sinterklaas

    Made into animals and topical figures.

    Key ingredients Almonds, sugar

    How it is eaten Small figures

  • Kerststol · Christmas stollen

    Where NL – national

    When Christmas brunch

    Has a German origin; buttered versions since the 15th century.

    Key ingredients Raisins, currants, peel, almond paste

    How it is eaten Buttered slices at brunch

  • Kerstkransje · Christmas ring biscuit

    Where NL – national

    When Christmas

    Ring-shaped butter biscuits with almonds, often hung in the Christmas tree on a ribbon.

    Key ingredients Butter dough, almonds

    How it is eaten With coffee

  • Tulband · Bundt cake

    Where NL – national

    When Christmas

    Called a Christmas classic.

    Key ingredients Flour, butter, raisins

    How it is eaten Slices with powdered sugar

  • Rollade · Rolled pork roast

    Where NL – national

    When Christmas

    A spiced pork roll without filling.

    Key ingredients Pork, spices

    How it is eaten Hot slices with gravy

  • Gourmetten · Tabletop grill meal

    Where NL – national

    When Christmas, New Year

    Each person cooks small portions on a table grill.

    Key ingredients Small meats, fish, vegetables, sauces

    How it is eaten Shared at the table, hours long

  • Oliebollen · Dutch doughnut balls

    Where NL – national

    When 31 December; kermis

    First known recipe as "oliekoecken" in De verstandige kock (1667); called oliebollen from the late 19th century.

    Key ingredients Yeast dough, raisins, currants, apple

    How it is eaten Hot, dusted with powdered sugar

  • Appelflappen · Apple turnovers

    Where NL – national

    When 31 December

    Part of the New Year's Eve baking.

    Key ingredients Puff pastry, apple, raisins, cinnamon

    How it is eaten Warm with sugar

  • Appelbeignets · Apple fritters

    Where NL – national

    When 31 December

    New Year's Eve fritters; also pineapple.

    Key ingredients Apple rings, batter

    How it is eaten Hot with sugar

  • Speculoos (Belgisch) · Belgian speculoos

    Where BE – national; Brussels (Dandoy)

    When Sinterklaas (6 Dec), coffee

    Traditionally baked for Saint Nicholas Day.

    Key ingredients Cinnamon, clove, brown sugar

    How it is eaten With coffee

    Protected VLAM label: Belgische speculaas, Hasseltse speculaas

  • Driekoningentaart · Three Kings cake

    Where BE – Flanders

    When 6 January

    Whoever finds the bean is king for the day.

    Key ingredients Puff pastry, almond cream, a bean

    How it is eaten Shared slices, with a paper crown

  • Derthienkoeck · Leuven Epiphany cake

    Where BE – Leuven

    When Around 6 January

    Shared out at the Groot Begijnhof of Leuven.

    Key ingredients Flour, yeast, sugar, cinnamon, milk, egg

    How it is eaten Shared

    Protected Flemish ICH list

  • Klaaskoeken · Saint Nicholas buns

    Where BE – Flanders

    When Sinterklaas (6 Dec)

    Sweet bread figures given to children for Sinterklaas on 6 December.

    Key ingredients Sweet dough

    How it is eaten For children

  • Cougnou / kerstbrood (Jezuke) · Christmas bread

    Where BE – national

    When Christmas

    Shaped like the baby Jesus.

    Key ingredients Sweet dough, sugar

    How it is eaten Breakfast on Christmas

🇩🇪 German, Austrian and Swiss food

  • Lübecker Marzipan · Lübeck marzipan

    Where DE – Lübeck, Schleswig-Holstein

    When Christmas, gifts

    Lübeck's marzipan tradition goes back centuries; Niederegger was founded in 1806. Only marzipan from Lübeck, Bad Schwartau and Stockelsdorf may use the name.

    Key ingredients Almonds, sugar (high almond share)

    How it is eaten As loaves, figures and marzipan pigs at New Year

    Protected PGI (1996)

  • Bremer Klaben · Bremen fruit loaf

    Where DE – Bremen

    When Christmas

    A heavy Stollen-type Christmas loaf; registered as a PGI by Commission Regulation (EC) No 1181/2009.

    Key ingredients Wheat dough, butter, raisins, sultanas, candied peel

    How it is eaten Thin slices, often buttered

    Protected PGI

  • Heidesand · Browned-butter shortbread

    Where DE – Lower Saxony

    When Advent

    A crumbly butter biscuit from northern Germany; the name is usually linked to the sandy soil of the Lüneburg Heath.

    Key ingredients Browned butter, sugar, flour

    How it is eaten With tea or coffee in Advent

  • Dresdner Christstollen · Dresden Christmas stollen

    Where DE – Dresden, Saxony

    When Advent, Christmas

    First recorded on a 1474 hospital bill; the 1491 papal "Butterbrief" allowed butter during Advent.

    Key ingredients Yeast dough, butter, raisins, almonds, candied peel, sugar

    How it is eaten Sliced through the Advent weeks with coffee

    Protected PGI

  • Pulsnitzer Pfefferkuchen · Pulsnitz gingerbread

    Where DE – Pulsnitz, Saxony

    When Advent

    The small town of Pulsnitz has kept a gingerbread bakers' tradition for centuries.

    Key ingredients Honey, rye and wheat flour, spices; often chocolate-coated or filled

    How it is eaten As Spitzen, hearts and filled pieces

  • Salzwedeler Baumkuchen · Salzwedel tree cake

    Where DE – Salzwedel, Saxony-Anhalt

    When Christmas, gifts

    Built up layer by layer on a rotating spit, showing rings like a tree trunk.

    Key ingredients Butter, eggs, sugar, flour, glaze; layered on a spit

    How it is eaten Cut across into thin rings

    Protected PGI

  • Reibekuchen / Kartoffelpuffer / Reiberdatschi · Potato pancakes

    Where DE – Rhineland (Reibekuchen), all

    When Autumn, Christmas markets, fairs

    The Cologne name is Reibekuchen, the Bavarian one Reiberdatschi.

    Key ingredients Grated raw potato, onion, egg; fried

    How it is eaten With apple sauce, or on dark bread with sugar beet syrup

  • Weckmann / Stutenkerl · Sweet bread man

    Where DE – Rhineland, Westphalia

    When St. Martin (11 Nov), Nikolaus (6 Dec)

    Given to children at Martinmas or St Nicholas; the clay pipe is thought to have once been a bishop's crozier.

    Key ingredients Sweet yeast dough, raisins, clay pipe

    How it is eaten Given to children at lantern parades

  • Aachener Printen · Aachen spiced biscuits

    Where DE – Aachen (NRW)

    When Advent, Christmas

    Hard spiced biscuits that may only be made in Aachen and certain neighbouring communities.

    Key ingredients Sugar beet syrup, flour, spices, candied sugar

    How it is eaten Nibbled with coffee; also used in Sauerbraten sauce

    Protected PGI

  • Spekulatius · Spiced windmill biscuits

    Where DE – Rhineland, Westphalia (also NL, BE)

    When Advent, Nikolaus

    Pressed into carved wooden moulds and linked with St Nicholas.

    Key ingredients Flour, butter, brown sugar, spices; printed with carved moulds

    How it is eaten With coffee or tea in Advent

  • Bethmännchen · Frankfurt marzipan cookies

    Where DE – Frankfurt

    When Advent

    Named after the Frankfurt banking family Bethmann; the story that they were created in 1838 is disputed, and they are probably older.

    Key ingredients Marzipan, almonds, rosewater

    How it is eaten With coffee in Advent

  • Springerle · Anise picture biscuits

    Where DE – Swabia, Baden; also Bavaria, CH (Anisbrötli)

    When Advent

    Pressed with carved wooden moulds that show pictures, then dried so they rise with a "foot".

    Key ingredients Eggs, sugar, flour, aniseed; carved wooden moulds

    How it is eaten Kept until Christmas to soften

  • Karpfen gebacken / Karpfen blau · Fried or blue carp

    Where DE – Franconia (Aischgrund), Upper Palatinate; AT Waldviertel

    When "Months with an R" (Sep–Apr); Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve

    Carp ponds date back to monastic fish farming; Franconian and Upper Palatinate carp are PGIs.

    Key ingredients Carp; breaded and fried, or poached in vinegar stock ("blau")

    How it is eaten Half a carp, with potato salad or brown bread

    Protected Aischgründer, Fränkischer, Oberpfälzer Karpfen PGI

  • Nürnberger Lebkuchen (Elisenlebkuchen) · Nuremberg gingerbread

    Where DE – Nuremberg

    When Advent, Christmas

    Protected as a PGI since 1 July 1996; "Elisen" quality requires a high nut share.

    Key ingredients Nuts and almonds, honey or sugar, spices, candied peel, wafer base

    How it is eaten Round cakes, plain, glazed or chocolate-coated

    Protected PGI

  • Kartoffelknödel (Klöße) · Potato dumplings

    Where DE – Bavaria, Franconia, Thuringia, Saxony

    When Sunday, Christmas

    Bavarian, Franconian and Thuringian styles differ in their ratio of raw to cooked potato (c for Thuringia).

    Key ingredients Raw and or cooked potatoes, croutons inside

    How it is eaten With roasts, goose and red cabbage

    Protected Bamberger Hörnla potato PGI

  • Rinderrouladen · Beef roulades

    Where DE – all

    When Sunday, Christmas

    Beef slices rolled around bacon, onion and gherkin, tied with string or pinned with toothpicks, then braised.

    Key ingredients Thin beef slices, mustard, bacon, onion, gherkin, red-wine gravy

    How it is eaten With red cabbage and Klöße or potatoes

  • Gänsebraten / Martinsgans · Roast goose

    Where DE – all; AT (Martinigansl)

    When 11 Nov (St. Martin), Christmas Day

    Explanations include the last feast before the Advent fast, rents paid in geese on 11 Nov, and the legend of geese giving away St. Martin's hiding place.

    Key ingredients Goose, apples, onion, mugwort (Beifuß), marjoram

    How it is eaten With red cabbage, Klöße and gravy

  • Weihnachtsente · Christmas roast duck

    Where DE – all

    When 25–26 Dec

    Poultry dominates German Christmas Day meals.

    Key ingredients Duck, apples, oranges, marjoram

    How it is eaten With red cabbage and dumplings

  • Kartoffelsalat mit Würstchen · Potato salad with sausages

    Where DE – all (strongest in the East)

    When Heiligabend (24 Dec), parties

    Germany's most common Christmas Eve dish: 26% (2015) and 32% (2024) in YouGov polls, nearly half in the East.

    Key ingredients Potatoes; mayonnaise (North and East) or broth-vinegar (South); Wiener or Frankfurter sausages

    How it is eaten Salad made a day ahead, sausages warmed in water

  • Rehrücken / Wildbraten · Saddle of venison, game roast

    Where DE, AT – forest regions

    When Autumn hunting season, Christmas

    Roast saddle of venison or other game; Rehrücken Baden-Baden is a classic preparation.

    Key ingredients Venison or wild boar, juniper, red wine, cream sauce

    How it is eaten With Spätzle or Knödel, red cabbage, cranberry-filled pear

  • Rotkohl / Blaukraut · Braised red cabbage

    Where DE – all; AT

    When Autumn, winter, Christmas

    Called Rotkohl in the North and Blaukraut in the South.

    Key ingredients Red cabbage, apples, vinegar, cloves, bay, lard

    How it is eaten With goose, duck, Rouladen, Sauerbraten

  • Neujahrsbrezel · New Year's pretzel

    Where DE – Pfalz, Baden, Swabia, Rhineland-Palatinate

    When 31 Dec to 1 Jan

    A large sweet pretzel given for good luck at New Year.

    Key ingredients Sweet yeast dough, often braided

    How it is eaten Shared at New Year breakfast

  • Mohnkuchen · Poppy seed cake

    Where DE – Silesia, Saxony; AT

    When Sunday, Christmas

    Poppy seed cakes belong to Silesian, Bohemian, Austrian and German baking and remain popular in Saxony.

    Key ingredients Ground poppy seed, milk, semolina, yeast or shortcrust

    How it is eaten With coffee

    Protected Waldviertler Graumohn PDO (AT)

  • Zimtsterne · Cinnamon stars

    Where DE, AT, CH – all

    When Advent

    Among the most popular Christmas biscuits; in Switzerland only documented from the second half of the 19th century.

    Key ingredients Ground almonds or hazelnuts, egg-white meringue, cinnamon

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Vanillekipferl · Vanilla crescents

    Where AT, DE (Bavaria, Bohemia origin)

    When Advent

    Of Viennese and Bohemian origin.

    Key ingredients Ground nuts, butter, flour, vanilla sugar

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Spitzbuben / Linzer Augen · Jam sandwich biscuits

    Where DE, AT, CH

    When Advent

    In Switzerland traditionally baked with three round holes in the top.

    Key ingredients Shortcrust, red currant jam, icing sugar

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Pfeffernüsse · Pepper nuts

    Where DE – all

    When Advent

    Small glazed spice biscuits.

    Key ingredients Honey or syrup dough, pepper, spices, sugar glaze

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Dominosteine · Domino cubes

    Where DE – Dresden origin

    When Advent

    Said to have been created by Dresden chocolatier Herbert Wendler in 1936.

    Key ingredients Lebkuchen base, fruit jelly, marzipan, chocolate

    How it is eaten From the Christmas plate

  • Kokosmakronen / Makronen · Coconut or almond macaroons

    Where DE – all

    When Advent

    Easy macaroons on wafer bases.

    Key ingredients Egg white, sugar, coconut or almonds, wafer

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Butterplätzchen / Ausstecherle · Cut-out butter cookies

    Where DE – all

    When Advent (family baking)

    The cut-out biscuits children bake and decorate.

    Key ingredients Butter shortcrust, icing, sprinkles

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Früchtebrot / Hutzelbrot / Kletzenbrot · Dried-fruit bread

    Where DE – Swabia, Bavaria; AT (Kletzenbrot); CH (Birnbrot)

    When Advent, Christmas

    A dark bread packed with dried pears, plums and nuts. Baking traditionally began in late November or around St Thomas's Day (21 Dec); the first loaf was cut on Christmas Eve or St Stephen's Day.

    Key ingredients Dried pears (Hutzeln, Kletzen), figs, nuts, rye dough

    How it is eaten Thin slices with butter

  • Gebrannte Mandeln · Candied roasted almonds

    Where DE, AT – all

    When Christmas markets, fairs, Oktoberfest

    Almonds coated in caramelised sugar, made fresh and sold warm at market stalls; recipes go back to the 18th century.

    Key ingredients Almonds, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla

    How it is eaten Warm from a paper cone

  • Linzer Torte · Linz torte

    Where AT – Linz, Upper Austria

    When Advent, Christmas, year-round

    Often called the oldest named cake in the world, with a recipe dated 1653.

    Key ingredients Nut-and-butter dough, red currant jam, lattice

    How it is eaten In slices with coffee; keeps for days

  • Waldviertler Karpfen gebacken · Waldviertel fried carp

    Where AT – Waldviertel

    When Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve, autumn

    Baked (fried) carp from centuries-old fish ponds is in the register.

    Key ingredients Carp from Waldviertel ponds, breadcrumbs

    How it is eaten With potato or mayonnaise salad

  • Luzerner Chügelipastete · Lucerne vol-au-vent

    Where CH – Lucerne

    When Sundays, Christmas, feasts

    Lucerne's festive pie.

    Key ingredients Puff pastry case, veal or pork meatballs (Chügeli), mushrooms, raisins, cream sauce

    How it is eaten Served whole, then cut, with rice or peas

  • Grittibänz · St. Nicholas bread man

    Where CH – all (Grättimaa, Elggermaa by region)

    When 6 Dec (Samichlaus)

    Bread figures given on St. Nicholas Day.

    Key ingredients Zopf dough, raisins for eyes and buttons

    How it is eaten Torn by hand, for breakfast or a snack

  • Dreikönigskuchen · Three Kings' cake

    Where CH – all

    When 6 Jan

    Revived nationwide from 1952 by bread researcher Max Währen, with a recipe from the Lucerne bakery school; the finder of the king wears the crown.

    Key ingredients Sweet yeast dough "flower" of balls, almonds, a hidden plastic king

    How it is eaten Each tears off a ball; whoever finds the king is king for the day

  • Birnbrot / Birnweggen · Pear bread

    Where CH – Graubünden, Glarus, Toggenburg; Lucerne (Birnweggen)

    When Advent, Christmas, winter

    Main variants are the Bündner, Glarner and Toggenburger Birnbrot and the rolled Luzerner Birnweggen; recorded by Kulinarisches Erbe der Schweiz.

    Key ingredients Dried pears, nuts, raisins, spices, wrapped in bread dough

    How it is eaten Thin slices with butter

  • Basler Läckerli · Basel honey biscuits

    Where CH – Basel

    When Advent, gifts, year-round

    The first written Swiss Läckerli recipe is dated to 1621; the name may come from Old High German "leckon" (to lick).

    Key ingredients Honey, almonds, hazelnuts, candied peel, Kirsch (alcohol), sugar glaze

    How it is eaten Small squares with tea or coffee

  • Zürcher Tirggel · Zürich honey wafer

    Where CH – Zürich

    When Advent, Christmas

    Recorded in a 1461 Zürich court record as "Dirggely"; the honey biscuit of Zürich.

    Key ingredients Honey, flour; pressed in picture moulds, baked very hot

    How it is eaten Thin and hard, broken and let melt in the mouth

  • Mailänderli · Swiss butter cookies

    Where CH – all

    When Advent

    Early recipes appear as "Gâteau de Milan" in 18th–19th century Bern and Basel cookbooks; a link to Milan is unproven. Among the most popular Swiss Christmas cookies.

    Key ingredients Butter, sugar, eggs, flour, lemon zest

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Brunsli · Chocolate-almond cookies

    Where CH – Basel, all

    When Advent

    The name comes from "bruun" (brown); strongly linked to Basel.

    Key ingredients Ground almonds, chocolate, sugar, egg white, cinnamon

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Chräbeli · Anise "claw" cookies

    Where CH – all

    When Advent

    Said to have been a cheaper substitute for expensive marzipan.

    Key ingredients Eggs, sugar, flour, aniseed

    How it is eaten From the Christmas tin

  • Appenzeller Biberli · Appenzell filled gingerbread

    Where CH – Appenzell, St. Gallen

    When Year-round, Christmas

    A small honey cake with almond filling, often decorated with pressed pictures.

    Key ingredients Honey gingerbread dough, almond filling

    How it is eaten With coffee

  • Schenkeli · Little thighs (fried biscuits)

    Where CH – all

    When Fasnacht, Christmas

    Small cylindrical fried biscuits; the name comes from 'Schenkel' (thigh), in French 'cuisses de dames'.

    Key ingredients Butter, sugar, eggs, flour, Kirsch (alcohol) or lemon; fried

    How it is eaten Eaten by hand

  • Magenbrot · "Stomach bread" spiced cakes

    Where CH, DE, AT – fairs

    When Fairs (Chilbi, Kirmes), Christmas markets

    Sold in paper bags at fairs and markets.

    Key ingredients Gingerbread dough pieces, chocolate glaze

    How it is eaten From a paper bag

  • Kinderpunsch · Children's punch

    Where DE, AT – all

    When Advent, Christmas markets

    The alcohol-free version of mulled wine for children at Christmas markets.

    Key ingredients Fruit tea, apple or red grape juice, orange, cinnamon, cloves

    How it is eaten Hot, in market mugs

🇪🇸 Spanish and Latin American food

  • Pozole · Hominy stew
