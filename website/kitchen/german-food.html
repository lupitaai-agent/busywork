Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

German, Austrian and Swiss food: the dishes people cook at home

Bread, the Sunday roast and coffee with cake.

German-language cooking is not one national cuisine but a family of regional kitchens, from the North Sea to the Alps: Rhenish, Swabian, Bavarian, Viennese, Tyrolean and Swiss. Borders cut across them. Bavaria and Austria share their dumplings; Swabia, Vorarlberg and eastern Switzerland share their Spätzle.

Everything starts with bread. German bread culture is on the national heritage list, and the bread register counts more than 3,000 kinds. Long winters taught people to keep food: sauerkraut, pickles, smoked and air-dried meats and the stored fruit of autumn. Christmas brings the great baking season: Stollen, Lebkuchen and dozens of biscuits.

The table has a steady rhythm. Breakfast is rolls with butter and jam. The Sunday roast, with dumplings and red cabbage, is the week's feast. In the afternoon comes Kaffee und Kuchen, coffee and cake at the table, often with the whole family. The evening is Abendbrot: bread, cheese and cold meats. In Vienna the coffee house is a living room for the city; in the Alps, the cheese melts by the fire.

How to say it

Spätzle (SHPETS-leh), Kaiserschmarrn (KY-zer-shmarn), Abendbrot (AH-bent-broht).

How people eat

  • Frühstück (breakfast) Bread rolls (Brötchen, Semmel, Schrippe, Weck, Weggli) with butter, jam, honey, cheese and cold cuts, and a boiled egg on Sundays. In Switzerland the Sunday Zopf ( for its "Sunday" link). Bavaria has the late-morning Weißwurstfrühstück: Weißwurst, sweet mustard and Brezn, by custom eaten before the noon bell, a rule from before refrigeration.
  • Mittagessen (lunch) Traditionally the hot main meal of the day, and still so in much of the South and in canteens.
  • Sonntagsbraten The Sunday roast (Sauerbraten, Rouladen, Schweinsbraten) with Klöße or Knödel and red cabbage, after church, as the family's weekly feast. I found no dedicated scholarly source (gap). Austria's equivalent is Schweinsbraten or Tafelspitz; Switzerland's is a Braten or Chügelipastete with Zopf.
  • Kaffee und Kuchen An afternoon coffee and cake ritual, usually between 3 and 5 pm, especially on Sundays and with visitors. Real coffee and home-baked cake became the Sunday ritual after the industrial era and the ersatz coffee (Muckefuck) of the war years. The Bergisches Land has its own elaborate "Bergische Kaffeetafel".
  • Abendbrot A cold evening meal of bread, butter, cheese, sausage and pickles. Cultural researchers date it to around the 1920s, and it spread after 1945 as more women went out to work. Surveys show it is still popular but declining as more people eat a warm dinner.
  • Brotzeit / Vesper / Jause / Znüni and Zvieri Snack meals: Bavarian Brotzeit (Brezn, Radi, Obatzda, Leberkäse), Swabian Vesper (bread, Schwarzwälder Schinken, Wurstsalat), Austrian Jause and Brettljause (cold meats, Speck, Kren, cheese, Verhackert on a wooden board), and the Swiss Znüni (mid-morning) and Zvieri (afternoon).
  • Wiener Kaffeehaus You sit for hours over one coffee. The water glass is refilled, newspapers hang on holders, the waiter is "Herr Ober", and coffee is ordered by type (Melange, Einspänner, Verlängerter, Kleiner Brauner). It is listed as national intangible heritage in Austria since 2011, recognised as a whole culture of ritual, atmosphere and hospitality rather than as buildings.
  • Raclette and Fondue evenings In Switzerland, Fondue (one shared caramelised pot, bread on long forks, the "Grossmutter" crust at the bottom) and Raclette (cheese scraped from a half-wheel, or table grills with small pans) are winter social meals. Table-top Raclette and Fondue are also among Germany's most typical Silvester (New Year's Eve) dinners.
  • Heiligabend simplicity Kartoffelsalat mit Würstchen is Germany's most common Christmas Eve dish: 26% in a 2015 YouGov poll (nearly half in the East) and 32% in November 2024. Poultry (duck, goose) dominates on 25 and 26 December. Austria and Catholic Bavaria have a tradition of carp or fish on 24 December.
  • Beer garden, Heuriger and Besen Bring-your-own-food traditions in Bavarian beer gardens, and simple cold food at Austrian Heurigen and Swabian Besenwirtschaften. The drinks there are alcoholic and outside this catalogue.
  • Schlachtfest and Kirchweih/Kirta Village slaughter feasts (fresh sausages, Wellfleisch, Panhas) and church-anniversary fairs with Kirchweihnudeln and Auszogne.

In the cupboard

  • Grains and bread Rye (sourdough), wheat, spelt (Dinkel) and its green-harvested form Grünkern, barley (Graupen; Bündner Gerstensuppe), oats, buckwheat (Styrian Heidensterz, North German Buchweizenpfannkuchen), cornmeal in the Rhine valley (Riebel/Ribel) and Styria, and poppy seed (Waldviertler Graumohn).
  • Potatoes Mealy and waxy types are used for different dishes. Protected: Bamberger Hörnla (PGI).
  • Cabbage family and roots White cabbage (sauerkraut, Filderkraut PGI), red cabbage, kale (Grünkohl), kohlrabi, swede (Steckrübe), Teltower Rübchen, horseradish (Bayerischer Meerrettich PGI, Steirischer Kren PGI), salsify, celeriac, onions (Höri Bülle PGI).
  • Seasonal stars White asparagus (season ends on Johannistag, 24 June, called "Spargelsilvester"), chanterelles (Eierschwammerl), wild garlic, rhubarb, strawberries, plums (Zwetschgen), apricots (Wachauer Marille PDO), cherries, apples, pears (dried as Kletzen, Hutzeln or Birnen), walnuts, chestnuts (Swiss Vermicelles), pumpkin.
  • Meat Pork dominates (roasts, knuckle, cured and smoked cuts, sausages), followed by beef (Tafelspitz, Rouladen, Sauerbraten), veal (Wiener Schnitzel, Zürcher Geschnetzeltes), goose and duck for feasts, game (deer, wild boar, hare) in autumn, and heath lamb in the north.
  • Fish North: herring (Matjes, Rollmops, Bismarck), plaice, eel, sprats. Inland: carp (Franconia, Upper Palatinate, Waldviertel), trout (Schwarzwaldforelle PGI), pike-perch, whitefish (Felchen) on the Swiss and Lake Constance lakes.
  • Dairy Quark/Topfen, butter, cream, buttermilk, many hard and mountain cheeses, sour-milk cheeses (Harzer, Handkäse, Tiroler Graukäse, Glarner Schabziger), Heumilch (TSG).
  • Fats and oils Lard and Grammeln/Grieben, butter, Butterschmalz, linseed oil (Lausitz, Spreewald), Styrian pumpkin-seed oil (PGI).
  • Sweet and spice Honey, beet sugar, almonds and marzipan, hazelnuts, poppy seed, cinnamon, cloves, aniseed (Springerle, Chräbeli), cardamom, nutmeg, candied citrus peel (Lebkuchen, Stollen), caraway (bread, roasts, sauerkraut), juniper and bay (sauerkraut, game), marjoram (sausages, potato soup), sweet and medium mustards.

  • Aschermittwoch Heringsessen (herring dinner)
  • Lent Fasting foods: Brezel, Maultaschen (legend), fish, Spinat
  • Gründonnerstag Frankfurter Grüne Soße, spinach with eggs, green herb dishes
  • Karfreitag Fish
  • Ostern Lamb roast; Osterlamm cake; Osterzopf and Osterbrot; dyed eggs; Kärntner Osterjause and Fleischweihe (Reindling, ham, Kren, eggs); Steirische Osterpinze; Osterfladen (Basel)
  • mid-Apr to 24 Jun Spargelzeit, ending on Johannistag ("Spargelsilvester")
  • May–Jun Strawberries, rhubarb cake, new Matjes (June), elderflower syrup
  • Summer Rote Grütze, berries; Birnen, Bohnen und Speck (Hamburg, late summer); grilling with Rostbratwurst; Apfelschorle
  • Aug–Sep Zwetschgenkuchen and Datschi, Zwetschgenknödel; chanterelles; Marillenknödel (Wachau, July)
  • late Sep–early Oct Oktoberfest season: Wiesn-Hendl, Schweinshaxe, Ochsenbraten, Steckerlfisch, Brezn, Obatzda, Weißwurst, gebrannte Mandeln, Lebkuchenherzen
  • Sep–Oct Zwiebelkuchen (with new wine, Federweißer, not catalogued), harvest festivals, Kirchweih and Kirta pastries, game, pumpkin, Vermicelles (CH), carp season starts ("months with an R")
  • 11 Nov, Martinstag Martinsgans or Martinigansl (DE, AT), Weckmann and Stutenkerl at lantern parades
  • Advent Plätzchen baking; Stollen, Lebkuchen, Printen, Spekulatius, Springerle; Christmas markets (Striezelmarkt Dresden, Christkindlesmarkt Nürnberg); Kinderpunsch
  • 6 Dec, Nikolaus Stutenkerl and Weckmann (DE), Grittibänz (CH), nuts, mandarins, Lebkuchen
  • 24 Dec, Heiligabend Kartoffelsalat mit Würstchen (most common in DE); carp (Austria, Bavaria, the East); fondue chinoise in Switzerland
  • 25–26 Dec Goose, duck, Rouladen, game, Klöße, red cabbage; Christstollen with coffee (c for poultry dominance)
  • 31 Dec, Silvester Raclette or Fondue, Berliner (with a mustard-filled joke one), carp, herring salad, Bleigießen (custom, not food)
