Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

Italian food: the dishes people cook at home

Twenty regions, one table on Sunday.

🍳 Open Busy Kitchen free

Pictures made with AI, to show the kitchen's places and customs.

In December 2025 UNESCO made Italian cooking as a whole world heritage, the first national cuisine recognised that way: shared meals, respect for ingredients, nothing wasted and skills handed down.

Its story runs from the porridge and fish sauce of Rome, through Arab Sicily, where a geographer described dried pasta made near Palermo in 1154, to the courts and city-states of the Renaissance. Tomatoes arrived late and were printed in a sauce in 1692. In 1891 Pellegrino Artusi published a cookbook that grew from 475 to 790 recipes, many sent to him by readers, and it helped a young country cook together. Emigrants then carried pasta and tomatoes across the world, and made them a national symbol.

There are twenty regional kitchens. The north cooks with butter, rice and polenta; the south with olive oil, durum wheat and the sea. A meal has its order: antipasto, a primo of pasta or risotto, a secondo with its contorno, then something sweet. Sunday lunch can last all afternoon. Christmas Eve is meatless, and in the south it becomes a feast of fish. Cappuccino is for the morning.

How to say it

sfoglia (SFOL-yah), tortellini (tor-tel-LEE-nee), cotechino (koh-teh-KEE-noh).

Its regions

  • Valle d'Aosta Alpine, French-speaking. Fontina, butter, rye bread, and meat preserved for winter (carne salada, mocetta). Soups are made of layered bread and cheese. Fonduta, seupa à la vapelenentse, carbonade, tegole
  • Piemonte Rich court and farm kitchen. Egg-yolk pasta, truffles, long braises, a large antipasto spread, the hazelnut-chocolate tradition. The merenda sinoira ("the snack that becomes supper") is named as an ancestor of the aperitivo. Bagna càuda, agnolotti del plin, vitello tonnato, bonèt
  • Liguria Herbs, olive oil, focaccia, vegetable pies and preserved fish (stoccafisso). A seafaring kitchen: pandolce was built for voyages. Pesto, pansoti, focaccia di Recco, cappon magro
  • Lombardia Rice, butter, polenta, buckwheat in the Valtellina, pork and cabbage. Home of panettone. Risotto alla milanese, ossobuco, cassoeula, pizzoccheri, panettone
  • Trentino-Alto Adige Two kitchens. Trentino builds on polenta; Alto Adige is Tyrolean, with Knödel, speck and strudel. Canederli, strangolapreti, tortel di patate, strudel, zelten
  • Veneto Lagoon and plain. Rice and peas, stockfish (baccalà), sweet-sour fish, polenta, the Verona pandoro and the Treviso tiramisù. Risi e bisi, baccalà alla vicentina, sarde in saor, pandoro
  • Friuli Venezia Giulia A crossroads of Alpine, Slovene and Habsburg cooking. Montasio, San Daniele, sweet-savoury ravioli, fermented turnips. Cjarsons, frico, muset e brovade, gubana
  • Emilia-Romagna The fresh egg pasta heartland and the home of the AIC's deposited recipes. Parmigiano, prosciutto, mortadella, balsamic vinegar, piadina. Tortellini, tagliatelle al ragù, lasagne verdi, piadina
  • Toscana A frugal bread kitchen: stale-bread soups, beans, olive oil, grilled meat, game. Medieval Siena sweets. Ribollita, pappa al pomodoro, bistecca, panforte, cantucci
  • Umbria Inland: pork from Norcia, black truffle, lentils, griddle breads, Easter cheese breads. Pasta alla norcina, torta al testo, pizza di Pasqua, ciaramicola
  • Marche Between hills and sea: rich baked pasta, stuffed olives, fish stew (brodetto), spreadable salami. Vincisgrassi, olive all'ascolana, brodetto, frustingo
  • Lazio Rome's pecorino-and-guanciale pastas, offal (quinto quarto), artichokes, lamb. Carbonara, amatriciana, cacio e pepe, abbacchio, supplì
  • Abruzzo Shepherding and mountain cooking: mutton, guitar-cut pasta, saffron from L'Aquila, the many-course Vigilia. Chitarra, arrosticini, virtù, parrozzo
  • Molise A small, rural, deeply ritual kitchen: feast soups, fried sweets for named saints' days, Christmas Eve spelt pasta. Cavatelli, zuppa alla santè, calzone di San Giuseppe, sciusci
  • Campania The Naples kitchen: the long Sunday ragù, pizza, fried street food, the Christmas Eve of eel and salt cod, and huge feast pastry. Pizza napoletana, ragù, parmigiana, pastiera, struffoli
  • Puglia Durum wheat, olive oil, greens and legumes, bread from Altamura, fried street dough, almond and must sweets. Orecchiette, fave e cicorie, tiella, cartellate, pasticciotto
  • Basilicata A poor and nourishing mountain kitchen: dried crusco peppers, horseradish, legumes, Arbëreshë dishes. Strascinati con cruschi, rafanata, crapiata, calzoncelli
  • Calabria Chili and 'nduja, swordfish, aubergines, honey-and-fig sweets, Greek and Arab traces in the pastries. Fileja con 'nduja, pesce spada, pitta 'mpigliata
  • Sicilia The most layered kitchen: Arab, Norman, Spanish. Sweet-sour flavours, couscous, ricotta and almond sweets, fried street food. Pasta con le sarde, arancine, caponata, cannolo, cassata
  • Sardegna An island pastoral kitchen: pecorino, flatbread kept for weeks, suckling pig, semolina pasta, honey sweets. Culurgiones, fregola, porceddu, pane carasau, seadas

How people eat

  • Order of courses The formal sequence is antipasto, primo (pasta, risotto or soup), secondo (meat or fish) with a contorno (vegetable side, served alongside or after), then dolce. One source argues this structure settled only once pasta became everyday food. Before that, from Rome to the 20th century, meals were mostly antipasto, secondo and dessert. Today two courses are normal and four are not required. Pasta is not served as a side next to the meat on the same plate (inference from the course structure in).
  • Sunday lunch (pranzo della domenica) The long family meal built around a slow sauce or a baked pasta. The Neapolitan ragù, cooked for hours with large cuts of meat whose sauce dresses the pasta and whose meat becomes the secondo, is the clearest example.
  • Christmas Eve and fasting The 24 December dinner is di magro (meatless) by old church rule. In the centre and south it has become a large feast of fish. In the north the main meal is Christmas lunch. Some areas have fixed numbers of courses: seven meatless courses at the antivigilia in the Oltrepò Pavese, and 13 courses at Lanciano (Abruzzo) for Jesus and the 12 apostles. One Basilicata source also mentions 13 fish courses. A fixed Italian rule of "seven fishes" was not confirmed by Italian sources. It appears to be mainly an Italian-American framing.
  • Coffee rules Cappuccino is a breakfast drink and is not ordered after a meal or in the afternoon. The "not after 11 am" line is a common convention rather than a written rule, and it is applied more strictly in tourist guides than in real life. After meals people drink an espresso or a macchiato. Water comes before the coffee, not after. Standing at the bar counter is the norm and is cheaper than table service. In many bars you pay at the till first and hand the receipt to the barista. In Naples and the south, coffee is more often drunk sitting at a table.
  • Aperitivo The aperitivo as a ritual grew up in 19th-century Turin around vermouth. Food historians trace the idea to the Piedmontese Sunday merenda sinoira, "the snack that becomes supper". Non-alcoholic aperitivo sodas (bitter analcolici) were a 20th-century bar staple.
  • Merenda The mid-afternoon snack, especially for children. In Genoa it is focaccia, in Rome pizza bianca, elsewhere bread with butter and jam, or bread with oil and tomato.
  • Gifts and luck at the table Lentils on New Year's Eve stand for coins; the more you eat, the more money comes. In Genoa, the sausage pieces in Christmas natalini broth stand for palanche (old copper coins). At Molise's pizza scema on San Martino (11 November), hidden seeds hand out playful "penalties". On 19 March, the tavole di San Giuseppe in Puglia (Lecce, Taranto, Brindisi), Sicily, Abruzzo and Molise are tables laid by families and opened to the community and the poor.

In the cupboard

  • Grains and pasta Durum wheat semolina (dried pasta; Pasta di Gragnano IGP) and soft wheat with eggs (fresh sfoglia in Emilia, Piedmont and Marche; Maccheroncini di Campofilone IGP). Rice in the Po plain (Riso Vialone Nano Veronese IGP). Cornmeal polenta in the north. Buckwheat in the Valtellina (Pizzoccheri della Valtellina IGP). Spelt (Farro della Garfagnana IGP) and chickpea flour (farinata, cecina, panelle).
  • Bread Stale bread is a main ingredient in ribollita, pappa al pomodoro, canederli, passatelli, panzanella and the Valle d'Aosta soups. Protected breads include Pane Toscano DOP, Pane di Altamura DOP, Pane di Matera IGP, Pane casareccio di Genzano IGP, Piadina Romagnola IGP and Focaccia di Recco col formaggio IGP.
  • Fats Butter and lard in the north and Alps; extra-virgin olive oil in the centre and south. This is a broad pattern taken from the dish rows (inference).
  • Cheese Parmigiano Reggiano DOP, Pecorino Romano DOP, Fontina DOP, Montasio DOP, Asiago DOP, Taleggio DOP, Bitto DOP, Valtellina Casera DOP, Squacquerone di Romagna DOP, Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP, Mozzarella STG, Burrata di Andria IGP, Ricotta Romana DOP, Pecorino Toscano DOP, Pecorino Sardo DOP, Fiore Sardo DOP, Pecorino Siciliano DOP, Ragusano DOP, Caciocavallo Silano DOP, Provolone Valpadana DOP. Grana Padano and Gorgonzola are DOP.
  • Cured meats Prosciutto di Parma DOP, Prosciutto di San Daniele DOP, Mortadella Bologna IGP, Cotechino Modena IGP, Zampone Modena IGP, Speck Alto Adige IGP, Bresaola della Valtellina IGP, Ciauscolo IGP, Lardo di Colonnata IGP, Salama da sugo IGP, Coppia ferrarese IGP (bread), Porchetta di Ariccia IGP.
  • Meat Abbacchio Romano IGP, Agnello di Sardegna IGP, Vitellone Bianco dell'Appennino Centrale IGP (the cattle behind the bistecca).
  • Vegetables and legumes Pomodoro San Marzano dell'Agro Sarnese-Nocerino DOP, Basilico Genovese DOP, Carciofo Romanesco del Lazio IGP, Radicchio Rosso di Treviso IGP, Cipolla Rossa di Tropea Calabria IGP, Peperone di Senise IGP (dried as crusco), Lenticchia di Castelluccio di Norcia IGP, Fagiolo di Sarconi IGP; also cime di rapa, friarielli, cavolo nero, fava beans and cicerchia.
  • Fruit, nuts and sweet staples Pistacchio Verde di Bronte DOP, Nocciola Piemonte IGP, Arancia Rossa di Sicilia IGP, Mela Val di Non DOP, Fichi di Cosenza DOP, Liquirizia di Calabria DOP. Also almonds, honey, candied citron, and cooked grape must (mosto cotto, vincotto, saba), which sweetens many southern and Sardinian festive sweets.
  • Fish Salted anchovies, sardines, swordfish, and clams and arselle. Stockfish and salt cod (baccalà) are imported but central to Christmas Eve and Lent. Eel (capitone) is a Christmas Eve fish.
  • Spices and flavourings Zafferano dell'Aquila DOP, wild fennel, chili (Calabria, Basilicata), black pepper in panpepato, pisto spice in Neapolitan roccocò, orange-flower water in pastiera, and Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP.
