Busy Kitchen · the six kitchens

British, Irish and American food: the dishes people cook at home

The roast, the pie and the pot of tea.

🍳 Open Busy Kitchen free

Pictures made with AI, to show the kitchen's places and customs.

The English-language kitchen is really three: British, Irish and American. They share roasting, baking, pies, puddings and tea, and each was reshaped by climate, hard times, empire, migration and war.

In Britain the Sunday roast is the best-known meal: a joint with roast potatoes, vegetables and gravy, and with beef, a Yorkshire pudding, a name first printed in 1747. Pies and pasties fed farm, mine and mill workers; the Cornish pasty is now a protected name. Afternoon tea is usually credited to a duchess around 1840, though that single-founder story is disputed. The words for meals still tell you where someone is from: in the north, dinner is at midday and tea is the evening meal.

Ireland cooked over turf with soda bread and potatoes. America built its kitchen from Native foods such as corn, beans and squash, from African American cooks who gave the South its gumbo, greens and cornbread, and from every migrant who arrived. Thanksgiving gathers the whole family; Louisiana boils crawfish; New Mexico roasts its chiles every autumn.

How to say it

Yorkshire pudding (YORK-shuh), haggis (HAG-is), gumbo (GUM-boh).

How people eat

  • Britain
  • Sunday lunch / Sunday roast The week's main family meal, eaten early afternoon, usually at home or in the pub; leftovers become Monday's cold cuts, bubble and squeak or shepherd's pie. It is the single most recognised British home-cooking ritual.
  • The full breakfast Fried eggs, bacon, sausages, black pudding, beans, tomatoes, mushrooms, toast or fried bread; regional versions are the full English, full Scottish (with square sausage, tattie scones, haggis), full Welsh (laverbread, cockles), Ulster fry (soda farl, potato bread) and full Irish (white pudding). Now mostly a weekend or holiday meal.
  • Afternoon tea vs high tea Afternoon tea is a light occasion meal of sandwiches, scones with jam and cream and small cakes; 'high tea' was historically the working family's hot evening meal at a table. A cream tea (scones, clotted cream, jam, tea) is the South West version, with the Devon (cream first) and Cornwall (jam first) argument.
  • Meal names Breakfast, dinner (midday, in working-class and northern usage) or lunch, tea (evening meal in the north, Scotland, Wales, Ireland) and supper (a late snack or the evening meal in the south). 'Pudding' or 'afters' means dessert.
  • Tea itself Strong black tea with milk ('builder's tea') is drunk through the day; offering tea to a visitor is a basic courtesy.
  • Fish and chips on Friday, The chippy tea, the pub lunch, the ploughman's.
  • Christmas dinner (25 December, early afternoon): roast turkey with pigs in blankets, stuffing, bread sauce, cranberry sauce, roast potatoes, Brussels sprouts, gravy, then Christmas pudding with brandy butter or custard; crackers and paper hats.
  • Ireland
  • United States
  • Thanksgiving dinner (fourth Thursday in November): the year's largest family meal, with roast turkey, stuffing or dressing, mashed potatoes and gravy, cranberry sauce, sweet potatoes, green bean casserole, rolls and pumpkin, pecan or sweet potato pie; American football on television and a 'Friendsgiving' among friends.
  • Potlucks (church suppers, 'covered-dish' dinners, the Midwest's hotdish and Jell-O salad, Southern funeral food): every guest brings a dish to share.
  • Barbecue In the South, slow-smoked meat as social event (church, political rallies, family reunions); elsewhere, backyard grilling of burgers and hot dogs, especially on Memorial Day, the Fourth of July and Labor Day. South Carolina made barbecue its official 'picnic cuisine' in 2014.
  • Breakfast and brunch Pancakes or waffles with maple syrup, eggs, bacon, hash browns; in the South, biscuits and gravy and grits; Sunday brunch after church.
  • Supper vs dinner In rural and Southern usage 'dinner' is midday and 'supper' the evening meal; elsewhere 'dinner' is evening.
  • Game-day food Super Bowl Sunday (February) is one of the biggest home-eating days: wings, chili, dips, nachos, pizza; US estimates of wing consumption run to about 1.4 billion wings for the game ( and trade figures cited there).
  • African American family reunions, Sunday dinners and Juneteenth (19 June, federal holiday since 2021), with red foods and drinks as a symbol.
