# Brand health check (mental availability)

Goal: measure how many category buyers think of the business in buying situations, compared with competitors.

## Designing the survey
1. **Who**: category buyers in the area served, both customers and non-customers. Screen with "Have you bought [category] in the last [period]?" Aim for 30–100 answers; more is better.
2. **Brands**: the business plus 4–8 competitors, shown as a list (logos optional), plus "none of these".
3. **CEPs**: the 8–12 priority buying moments from the Brand Profile, phrased as situations: "Which of these would you think of when… [your chain snaps on the way to work]?" Allow multiple answers.
4. **Assets (optional)**: show each brand element without the name and ask which business it belongs to.
5. **Channels**: free tools (Google Forms, Typeform), local Facebook groups, in-store QR codes, a newsletter, a panel if budget allows. Avoid only surveying fans.

## The numbers
- **Mental penetration**: % of category buyers who linked the business to at least one CEP.
- **Network size**: among those, the average number of CEPs linked to the business.
- **Mental market share**: the business's links divided by all links to all brands.
- Per CEP: % linking each brand. Look for CEPs where the business is weak but the moment is common.

## Reading results
Compare with competitors' size: expect bigger brands to score higher on most CEPs (that's normal). Growth shows as rising mental penetration first. Repeat once or twice a year with the same wording.

## Output
A ready-to-use survey (questions + answer options) or, with results pasted, the four numbers, a CEP table and three actions. The Studio's Monitor tab can store each wave.
