# Competitive Battlecards

Build complete, field-ready competitive battlecards that give sales and marketing teams the
intel, messaging, and objection handling they need to win.

---

## When to Use This Skill

- Creating a new battlecard for a competitor (CoreWeave, Lambda, AWS, GCP, etc.)
- Refreshing cards after product updates, pricing changes, or win/loss data
- Training teams ahead of major deals or campaigns
- Building out a competitive enablement program from scratch
- Packaging differentiation guidance for field, SDR, or AE teams

---

## Step 1: Competitive Intel Capture

For each competitor, gather:

```markdown
### Competitor Profile: [Competitor Name]

**Strengths** (what they do well, where they win):
- 

**Weaknesses** (where they lose, common complaints):
- 

**Key Proof Points** (what they claim, with evidence):
- 

**Trigger Signals** (when prospects bring them up):
- "They offered us X" / "We already use them for Y" / "They are cheaper"

**Pricing & Packaging** (what is publicly known):
- 

**Recent Changes** (new features, pricing, messaging, executive moves):
- 
```

---

## Step 2: Card Structure

Each battlecard contains these sections:

```markdown
## [Competitor Name]: Battlecard

**Last updated**: [date] | **SME**: [owner] | **Confidence**: [High / Medium / Low]
**Segment**: [Enterprise / Mid-market / SMB] | **Stage**: [Awareness / Evaluation / Procurement]

---

### Overview (1 sentence)
[What they are and why prospects consider them]

### Our Positioning vs. [Competitor]
[Headline positioning statement: 1 to 2 sentences, "permission to win"]

### Where We Win
| Dimension | Us | Them |
|-----------|----|----|
| [e.g. delivery time] | | |
| [e.g. pricing model] | | |
| [e.g. bare-metal performance] | | |

### Landmines (what to plant in discovery)
- "How do you handle [X]?": surfaces their weakness without naming them
- 

### Talk Track
"When a prospect asks about [Competitor], here is how to respond:
[2 to 4 sentence script]"

### Objection Handling
| Objection | Response | Supporting Asset |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| "[Competitor] is cheaper" | | |
| "We already use [Competitor]" | | |
| "[Competitor] has [feature we do not]" | | |

### Proof Points
- [Customer quote / case study / benchmark / analyst quote]

### Trap-Setting (use carefully: flag with legal/product first)
- [Questions or framings that expose competitor weaknesses]

### Offer Packaging (if relevant)
- Recommended deal structure or trial offer when competing against this vendor
```

---

## Step 3: Messaging Pillars

Before writing card copy, align on:

```markdown
### Messaging Pillars for [Competitor]

1. **Headline differentiator**: [The single most important reason to choose us]
   - Supporting proof: [data point, case study, customer quote]
   - Objection response: [most common pushback]

2. **Secondary differentiator**: 
   - Supporting proof:
   - Objection response:

3. **Tertiary differentiator**:
   - Supporting proof:
   - Objection response:
```

Keep "permission to win" statements crisp: avoid feature dumping. One strong claim beats five
weak ones.

---

## Step 4: Metadata & Tagging

Tag every card so reps and tools can surface the right card at the right time:

```markdown
**Metadata**
- Competitor: [name]
- Segment: [Enterprise / Mid-market / SMB]
- Persona: [Economic buyer / Champion / Technical evaluator]
- Stage: [Awareness / Evaluation / Procurement / Renewal]
- Product module: [if multi-product]
- Last updated: [YYYY-MM-DD]
- SME / Owner: [name]
- Confidence level: [High / Medium / Low]
- Next review date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
- Localization: [regions where card applies]
```

---

## Step 5: Enablement Distribution

Make the cards accessible where reps actually work:

- **CRM surfaces** (Salesforce, HubSpot): embed as deal-stage-triggered cards
- **Slack channel**: `#competitive-intel` with searchable posts per competitor
- **Sales playbook**: section per competitor, linked to card
- **Onboarding**: include in new rep ramp materials
- **Outreach/Apollo sequences**: pull objection snippets into cadence templates
- **Content assets**: link to relevant case studies, benchmarks, comparison pages

---

## Step 6: Feedback Loop & Refresh Cadence

Battlecards go stale fast. Build a maintenance system:

```markdown
### Win/Loss Capture (after every competitive deal)
- Did we win or lose?
- Which competitor?
- What was the deciding factor?
- What objection came up that the card did not cover?
- What worked well?

### Refresh Schedule
- **Quarterly**: Full card review with SME
- **On trigger**: Competitor pricing/product/executive change
- **After 3+ losses to same competitor**: Emergency refresh

### Update Log
| Date | Owner | Change Summary |
|------|-------|---------------|
| | | |
```

---

## Output

Deliver:

1. **Completed battlecard** for each requested competitor (using card structure above)
2. **Messaging pillars doc** (if building from scratch)
3. **Objection handling matrix** (objection → response → asset → confidence level)
4. **Distribution checklist** (where the card needs to be added)
5. **Metadata tags** for each card
6. **Next review date** set

---

## Tips

- Align with legal and product teams before publishing sensitive competitive claims
- Include audio/video snippets from top performers where available
- Flag trap-setting questions clearly to avoid inadvertent disclosure
- Keep cards under 2 pages: if it is longer, it will not get used in the field
- "We are better at X" requires proof. "Customers switch to us because X" is stronger
