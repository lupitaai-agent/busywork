# Case Studies

The most credible content the business can produce is a customer who achieved something
real and will say so. This skill turns any customer input, a quote, an email, an interview,
rough notes: into a full case study asset package.

**Note:** never present an example in this skill as a real customer. Every case study needs a real customer, real numbers and their approval.

**System role**: Tier 1 content skill. Produces the highest-converting content type.
Feeds sales, investor relations, and the content library simultaneously.

---

- CTA: the business's main call to action (from the Brand Profile)

## Step 2: Customer Interview Guide

When the owner needs to capture a story from scratch or enrich a thin quote, use this question guide.
Adapt to the conversation, the goal is to get to the numbers and the before/after contrast.

### Opening (2 minutes)
- "Tell me what your marketing looked like before you started working with the business."
- "What was the specific problem you were trying to solve?"

### The Challenge (5 to 7 minutes)
- "What was the situation with your marketing (time, cost, consistency) before this?"
- "Had you tried other tools or an agency? What happened?"
- "What was the cost (in time, money, or missed opportunity) of the problem?"
- "What made this urgent enough to act on?"

### Why the business (3 to 5 minutes)
- "Why did you choose the business over the alternatives you were considering?"
- "What made the case internally to try it?"
- "Was it about cost, or was it something else: consistency, time back, visibility?"

### The Implementation (3 to 5 minutes)
- "How long did onboarding actually take?"
- "What did the rollout look like? Any surprises?"
- "What did you think once it was running?"

### The Results (5 to 10 minutes)
- "What changed once the business was running for you?"
- "What is the most concrete metric you can point to: time saved, content shipped, rankings, traffic?"
- "Compared to what you were doing before, what is the actual number?"
- "Has your business been able to do something it could not do before?"
- "Would you use the phrase [specific claim from their early feedback]? Is that accurate?"

### Looking Forward
- "What are you planning to use the business for next?"
- "What would you say to someone evaluating the business today?"

### Logistics
- "Are you comfortable with us using your company name?"
- "Would you be willing to do a short pull quote for the published piece?"
- "Is there anyone on your team I should copy on the draft for approval?"

## Step 3: Story Extraction

From the interview, transcript, or notes, extract:

```markdown
## Story Elements: [Customer Name/Alias]

### The Challenge
**Situation before the business:**
[What was their marketing setup / what tools or agency / what problem]

**The pain point:**
[Specific: the problem or moment that sent them looking, in their own words]

**Cost of the problem:**
[Numbers: $/month wasted, hours lost, content not shipped, missed rankings]

### The Hypothesis
**Why they tried the business:**
[What made them think the business would solve the problem]

**The decision criteria:**
[What they needed to see before committing]

**What they were comparing against:**
[What alternatives they evaluated: other tools, an agency, doing it in-house]

### The Implementation
**What they deployed:**
[What was delivered, what scope, as specific as the customer will allow]

**How long it took:**
[Onboarding time, time to first result]

**Any notable moments:**
[Anything that surprised them positively: speed, quality, control]

### The Change (Results)
**Primary metric:**
[The single best number: e.g., "published 3x more content with the same headcount," "cut time-to-publish in half"]

**Supporting metrics:**
[2 to 3 additional proof points]

**Qualitative shift:**
[What changed about what they could do: "we finally have visibility into what's actually happening in our marketing"]

**Direct quote (verbatim):**
["[exact words from customer]": [Name, Title, Company or "anonymous, SMB owner"]]
```

## Step 4: Write the Full Case Study

### Standard Structure (long-form, 600 to 1,000 words)

```
HEADLINE: How [Customer/Alias] [Achieved Specific Result] with the business

SUB-HEADLINE: [One sentence: the "before" and "after" in a single line]

EXEC SUMMARY (2 to 3 sentences):
[Who the customer is, what they needed to accomplish, what they achieved.
Lead with the result: bury the background.]

THE CHALLENGE
[2 to 3 paragraphs: describe the customer's situation, the specific pain, why it mattered.
Use first-person customer perspective where possible. Make the problem concrete: numbers
help here even if they are estimates.]

WHY [BUSINESS NAME]
[1 to 2 paragraphs: what made the business the right choice. Keep this honest: if
consolidating tools was the deciding factor, say so. If getting content that actually
sounded like their brand mattered most, explain why. Avoid generic praise.]

THE IMPLEMENTATION
[1 to 2 paragraphs: what they deployed, how long it took, what onboarding looked like.
Make the speed and simplicity tangible.]

THE RESULTS
[2 to 3 paragraphs: lead with the hardest number. Support with secondary metrics.
Include the verbatim customer quote here: do not paraphrase customer words.
End with what they can now do that they could not before.]

What is NEXT
[1 paragraph: what they are planning to use the business for next: signals ongoing
commitment and helps readers see their own potential path.]

CTA:
"Running a similar setup? Learn more at the business's website" (ask the owner)
```

### Checklist Before Delivery

- [ ] At least 2 hard metrics (numbers, not adjectives)
- [ ] At least 1 verbatim customer quote
- [ ] The business is named early and its distinctive assets are used in the visual formats
- [ ] Author attribution: the owner Herber, Founder, the business
- [ ] No invented details: every claim traceable to the interview
- [ ] Customer approved for use of their name (or alias clearly used)
- [ ] CTA points only to `the business's website`, no invented portal URL or email address

## Step 5: Format Variations

Once the long-form is approved, produce derivative formats:

### One-Pager (for sales/partnership use, ~200 words)

```
[Customer logo / alias]: [Industry/segment]

THE CHALLENGE:
[1 to 2 sentences on the problem]

THE RESULT:
[3 bullet points: hardest numbers first]
• [Primary metric]
• [Secondary metric]
• [Operational or team impact]

IN THEIR WORDS:
"[Verbatim customer quote]"
—  [Name, Title] or "[anonymous, SMB owner]"

THE SETUP:
[1 sentence on what they deployed and how fast]

---
[CTA: the business's website]
[the business logo]
```

### Email Snippet (for nurture sequences, ~80 words)

```
Subject line: How [Customer/Alias] [Result] with the business

[Customer/Alias], a [segment description], was [pain point in one line].
After bringing the business on, they achieved [primary result].

"[Short verbatim quote: 1 sentence]"

Full story → [link]

[CTA: the business's website]
```

### Social Tiles (3 variations)

Generate copy for each tile. the owner or the design skill handles visual production
(see the `visual-prompts` skill for on-brand image direction):

- **Tile 1 (The Number):** "[Primary metric]" — [Customer alias], [role]
- **Tile 2 (The Quote):** "[Verbatim quote, under 100 characters]" — [Customer alias]
- **Tile 3 (The Challenge/Result):** From [pain] to [result] in [timeframe]. [CTA link]

### LinkedIn Post (for the owner to publish under his name, or for the Studio's voice on brand social)

```
[Hook: the result number or most surprising fact]

[1 to 2 sentences: what the customer was trying to solve]

[2 to 3 sentences: what changed when they started running on the business]

"[Verbatim quote]"

[1 sentence: what this signals about running marketing for a small business today]

Full case study → [link]

#[YourCategory] #[YourTown] #SmallBusiness
```

## Step 7: Approval Workflow

Before anything goes public:

```markdown
### Approval Checklist

- [ ] **Customer has reviewed the draft**: share the long-form specifically
- [ ] **Customer approved verbatim quotes**: exact text, not paraphrased
- [ ] **Customer approved use of company name** (or alias confirmed as correct)
- [ ] **Legal has reviewed** if customer is a named business with its own brand exposure to consider
- [ ] **the owner has approved** final version before publishing
- [ ] **Distribution channels confirmed**: [portal / blog / email / sales use only / all]
```

If customer approval is pending, park the case study in the the Studio's saved results with status
"Awaiting Customer Approval" and do not publish.

## Save to Proof Library

After approval, append the core proof points to the Learnings log. This file is
currently empty or seeded only with placeholders, since the business has no customers yet:
this is the step that starts making it real.

```markdown
## [Customer Name/Alias]: [Date]
- **Segment**: [ICP]
- **Primary metric**: [X%/Xh/X$]
- **Quote**: "[Verbatim]": [Name/Title/Alias]
- **Full case study**: [URL or "in the Studio's saved results"]
- **Available formats**: [long-form / one-pager / social / email]
```

The proof library becomes the source of truth for the content-writer skill when it
needs social proof for future articles: "show me relevant proof points for a piece
targeting [segment]." Until the first real case study lands, content-writer should not
fabricate proof points in its place.
