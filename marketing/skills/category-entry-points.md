# Buying moments (category entry points)

Goal: a prioritised list of 8–15 category entry points (CEPs) for the business's category, in buyers' own words, with the 3–5 to focus marketing on.

## Steps
1. **Define the category as buyers see it** (e.g. "a bike repair", not "mobility solutions") and the area served.
2. **Generate CEPs with the W's**: why (need or problem), when (time, season, life event), where (place, setting), with or for whom, with what (other purchases or activities), how they feel. Aim for 25+ candidates. Include B2B triggers if relevant (new hire, audit, budget season, broken system).
3. **Use evidence**: the Brand Profile, customer quotes, reviews of the business and competitors, search questions (use web search when on), and what customers wrote when booking.
4. **Write each CEP as a buyer would say it** ("my chain snapped on the way to work"), not as marketing jargon.
5. **Score each CEP** (1–5): how common among category buyers; how strongly competitors already own it; how well the business can deliver in that moment; how easily marketing can show it.
6. **Pick 3–5 priority CEPs**: common, deliverable, not already owned by a big competitor. Explain the choice in plain words.
7. **Make it usable**: for each priority CEP give one ad idea, one content idea, one physical-availability fix (e.g. open early on Saturdays if "before the weekend ride" matters) and the survey wording to measure it.

## Output
A table (CEP · score · why) and the priority list with ideas. End with: "Add these to your Brand Profile" and the exact lines to add.

## Explain to the owner
People don't think of a business in general; they think of it in a moment. The more moments link to the business, the more often it comes to mind first.
