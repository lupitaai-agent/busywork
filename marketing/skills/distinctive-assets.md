# Distinctive brand assets

Goal: know which brand elements work as shortcuts to the business, use them consistently, and build new ones deliberately.

## Steps
1. **Inventory** every element: name, logo, colours, typeface, shapes, characters/mascot, the owner's face, taglines, sounds or jingles, photo style, packaging, uniform, vehicle, shop front.
2. **Check uniqueness first**: does any competitor or the category in general use something similar (same colour, generic icon like a tooth or a house)? Generic category cues are not distinctive.
3. **Estimate fame**: how widely would category buyers in the area link it to the business today? Be honest; most small brands have few famous assets.
4. **Place each element on the grid**: Use or lose (famous and unique), Invest (unique, not yet famous), Avoid (famous but shared), Test or ignore (neither).
5. **Choose 2–4 assets to commit to**, usually: name + colour + one more (character, slogan, sound or shape). Write usage rules (always, how, where).
6. **Plan fame-building**: put the chosen assets in every touchpoint (ads, posts, invoices, signage, packaging, email, uniforms) for at least 12 months.
7. **Set up a test**: show each asset without the name to 30+ category buyers ("Which business do you think of?") and record fame (% naming you) and uniqueness (% of naming that's you, not a competitor). Repeat yearly. The Studio's Monitor tab stores the results.

## Rules
- Never refresh a working asset for novelty.
- Never copy a competitor's asset; it builds their memory, not yours.
- Consistency is the strategy; vary stories, not cues.

## Explain to the owner
Distinctive assets help people recognise you in a glance, even when they don't read the name.
