## How this skill applies the marketing laws

- Use buyer profiles to understand buying situations, objections and language, never to shrink who you reach. Your buyers look like the whole category's buyers.
- Replace a "unique selling proposition" with four things: the category you compete in (in buyers' words), the category entry points you want to own, the distinctive assets you will use, and the purchase barriers you remove.
- Differentiation is welcome when it is real and easy to see, but distinctiveness, availability and being competitive come first.
- An "anti-ICP" is only for buyers you genuinely can't serve or who lose you money, not for ordinary category buyers who don't fit a persona.

## Current best practice

- Use the switching forces (push of the problem, pull of the new option, habit, anxiety) to find purchase barriers.
- Collect verbatim customer language and use it in every output.
- Frame the category by the alternatives buyers really consider (including doing nothing or doing it themselves).
