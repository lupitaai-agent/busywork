# Offers & promotions

Goal: use offers carefully, as activation, without damaging prices or brand.

## What the evidence says
- Price promotions mostly sell to people who would have bought anyway, and the sales lift ends when the promotion ends. They rarely create lasting growth.
- Frequent discounts lower the price people expect to pay.
- Offers that make *trying* easier for new buyers (a first-visit bundle, a free check, a trial size) remove purchase barriers and can help penetration.

## Steps
1. Define the goal: new buyers trying, filling quiet times, clearing stock, or rewarding loyalty.
2. Prefer value-adds and risk reducers (guarantee, free extra, bundle, trial) over straight price cuts.
3. Keep promotions rare, time-limited and clearly branded.
4. Estimate the cost: margin given away to people who would have bought anyway.
5. Measure: how many *new* customers it brought (from invoices), on top of revenue during the offer.

## Output
Two or three offer designs with expected cost and effect, the recommended one, and its copy.
